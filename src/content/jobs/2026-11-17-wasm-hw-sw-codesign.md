In our research group, we investigate how hardware/software co-design can improve the execution of WebAssembly on embedded RISC-V systems.

We are developing hardware accelerators that support different parts of WebAssembly interpreters and integrate them into a RISC-V-based System-on-Chip. Our work combines processor architecture, digital hardware, embedded software, and WebAssembly runtimes.

We are looking for a motivated student to support our work in one of these areas. Depending on your interests and previous experience, possible topics include:

- Developing and extending tests for existing hardware accelerators
- Investigating the interaction between the RISC-V processor, accelerators, and software
- Developing automated regression tests for hardware/software configurations
- Integrating selected accelerator functionality into the WebAssembly Micro Runtime (WAMR)
- Benchmarking and comparing software and hardware-assisted WebAssembly execution
- Automating experiments and result collection
- Analyzing the behavior of the accelerators for different WebAssembly workloads
- Supporting FPGA-based testing and evaluation of existing SoC designs

The exact topic is flexible and can be adapted to your interests. The work can focus more on digital hardware, embedded software, verification, performance evaluation, or automation.

What you should bring:

- Basic understanding of computer architecture and RISC-V
- Basic experience with digital hardware design and Verilog/SystemVerilog
- Programming experience, for example in C or Python
- Basic experience with Linux and Git
- Interest in hardware/software co-design and embedded systems

Nice to have:

- Experience with RTL simulation tools such as Verilator
- Experience with FPGA development
- Previous work with RISC-V SoCs
- Experience with embedded C
- Interest in WebAssembly or runtime systems

Previous experience with WebAssembly is not required.
