We are looking for a motivated student to join us for their master's thesis in the area of fault-injection simulation.

Properly functioning hardware relies on the correct processing of every bit. We create tools to investigate how hardware behaves under external influences such as radiation, aging, or targeted attacks, and whether a fault stays harmless, is detected, or silently produces wrong results. Since hardware designs have an extremely high number of possible states, we work on fast simulations that identify problematic designs before manufacturing.

As part of the [ISOLDE](https://aemy.cs.hm.edu/projects/isolde) project, we have extended the open-source simulator Verilator so that it can simulate faults in a given hardware design.

Possible thesis topics are:
- Investigating the security of RISC-V cores and their countermeasures against fault attacks
- Strategies for efficient fault-injection campaigns
- Simulating fault attacks on cryptographic cores
- Automated creation of fault models
- Analysis of fault-injection simulation results

If any of these topics sounds interesting to you, or if you have an idea for a related topic, feel free to reach out!
