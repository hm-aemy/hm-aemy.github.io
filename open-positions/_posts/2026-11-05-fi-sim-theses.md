We are looking for a motivated student wanting to write their Master's thesis in the area of fault-injection simulation.

Hardware must respond correctly even when individual bits flip, e.g. through radiation, aging, or targeted attacks. 
Whether such an error will have no consequences, be detected, or go unnoticed while producing incorrect results can be determined even before manufacturing begins through fault-injection simulation.

As part of the [ISOLDE](https://aemy.cs.hm.edu/projects/isolde) project, we have implemented an approach that enables Verilator to accurately simulate fault(-injection) in a provided hardware design.
Verilator is a powerful open-source tool for simulating digital hardware described in Verilog or SystemVerilog.

Possible thesis topics are:
- Optimization of fault-injection  simulation campaigns
- Fault(-injection) analysis of a RISC-V core and evaluation of protection mechanisms
- Simulation of fault-injection on cryptographic cores
- Automation for fault-model creation
- Analysis of resulting fault-injection simulation data

If any of these topics sounds interesting to you, or if you’ve been inspired and have a suggestion for a related topic, feel free to reach out!
